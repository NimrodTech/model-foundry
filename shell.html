How to read the floor

In the compact loop, the line runs in a snake from the back of the campus to the front. Straight line lays the same eleven stations end to end, with suppliers behind the line and people in front of it. Each station is one main machine with its name painted on the plate; plates are sized by how much time, compute and judgement the step takes. Belts carry the work: raw documents at first, then token shards, then, from the compute hall onward, the model itself as checkpoints. Trays in the floor carry everything else.

  • Data: documents, tokens and model checkpoints moving along the belts. The dashed belt from deployment back to fine-tuning is feedback from real use.
  • Energy: power from the utility island, along the floor bus to every station. The heavy feeds go to the compute hall and the inference racks.
  • Human judgement: every figure on the floor is a person making a call. Green trays bring in annotators, external evaluators and government testers.
  • Hardware: accelerators, memory and servers from the chip supply chain, into the compute hall, the ablation cluster and the inference racks.

Scale is compressed. One hall of 180 racks stands for clusters of 16,000 to 200,000+ GPUs, often spread over several buildings. Numbers in the panels are public figures from papers, model cards and reporting. Where a lab hasn't published, the text gives a range or says "estimated".
